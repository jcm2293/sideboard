# SESSION 4 — THE HATCH-DAY JOB
### A one-night heist of the Sanctum Bonafortunica, for six level-5 disasters · Run time 2.5–3 hours
*(The title is a spoiler only the crows understand: crows call a birthday a hatch-day, and they have been planning this job longer than Martenn has.)*

**The whole plot in three sentences:** Every cheat caught in Larracos's gambling houses loses their winnings *and* their cheating devices to the **Sanctum Bonafortunica** — a legendary vault whose location has bankrupted a generation of treasure hunters before they learned what *quadrant* it might be in. Martenn and Tobin have been planning to rob it for years, and every version of the plan dies at the same line: **NEED SIX.** Tonight, four broke, cursed, desperate-to-be-cool out-of-towners wander into the shop, and the math finally works.

**Design stance (yours):** simple spine; complexity comes from the players. There are only **four fixed points** tonight:
1. The entrance to the Sanctum is hidden beneath the floor of **the Full Fathom**, and Tobin — a Harrow — is the only person alive outside the family who knows it.
2. The antechamber door only opens for **cheaters** (the Honest Game).
3. The vault is guarded by **Heads and Tails** — and their coin can be cheated too.
4. It's Ren's birthday. She told no one. The crows know.

Everything else is **toys, not track.** Whatever plan the players invent is the right plan; your job is to price it, complicate it, and say yes.

**About the curse:** it gets exactly one mention in the recap — it's why they're in Larracos and can't go home — then it sleeps for the night. Ren's black stone stays in her pocket doing nothing. Someday it's how they'll know they can go home. Not today.

**Tonight is Ren's birthday in both worlds.** She turns seventeen today (which retroactively settles your 16-vs-17 continuity wobble — she was sixteen; as of today she isn't).

## PREVIOUSLY ON *SKINS* (read-aloud, Tavar voice)

> *"In the quarry town of Pellvar, statues of the living kept appearing, and the town council kept smiling about it. Four young people — a forge-boy with fire in his hands, a bard who did not know he was casting spells, a barbarian who answers his ancestors out loud, and a girl in black who had been right about everything the entire time — followed the trouble down a mine shaft and met Stenvala, the Stone Mother, four thousand years old and small enough to fit in a coin purse. They killed her. She did not go quietly: they are marked now, and if they ever walk back into Pellvar, they finish the statue collection.*
>
> *Councilwoman Adeline paid them fifty gold each to be someone else's problem, enchanted their weapons, and pointed at the horizon.*
>
> *Three weeks down the Southern Road later: Larracos. Second city of Balenthas. Thirty thousand souls, no walls, no plan, music that never stops — and the gambling capital of the known world."*

**Mara, if anyone asks (one line):** she walked partway with them, said she wanted to see where the deer trails actually go, and turned north. Friend, not fugitive. The crows speak well of her.

**Road montage (5 min):** around the table once — *"Three weeks on the road: one thing the road took from you, one thing you found."* One sentence each, no rolls. Warms the four up while Joe and Ilan absorb the tone.

**READ-ALOUD — first sight of Larracos:**

> *You smell Larracos before you see it — fish and tar, salt and pitch, frying meat, and under it all something older, like wet rope and luck. The Southern Road narrows between warehouses stacked four stories tall, a canyon of grain and hemp where the gulls scream lawsuits at each other overhead — and then you turn a corner and the world opens. A thousand masts against the evening. The temple of Ossuvar riding the end of the main pier in the ribcage of something enormous. The old elven aqueduct steaming gently, full of bathers. Lantern-light already climbing the hills, because Larracos does not wait for dark to begin — and music, from somewhere, from everywhere, braided out of a dozen doorways into one careless song. Nobody looks at you twice. You realize, all four of you, at the same moment: in Pellvar, everyone looked at you twice. You have been walking for three weeks to become nobody, and here it is, and you didn't expect it to sting.*

**Arrival color — make the city make them feel SMALL (this powers the whole pitch).** Pick 2–3, fast:
- A girl Ren's age sweeps past in blacks that were *purchased* — dyed silk, silver rings, professional eyeliner — gives Ren's boot-dyed cuffs one slow look, and says nothing. Says it loudly.
- A doorman at the Gilded Eel takes one look at Donovan's road clothes: "Deliveries in the back, farmhand." *Farmhand.* To DONOVAN.

- A gull robs a smoked tendon from Stookin's pouch, maintaining eye contact. Even the birds here think they're rubes. (Exception: a crow lands in front of Ren, sets down a polished bottlecap, says — clearly, in Common — **"It's you."** — and flies off like it wasn't supposed to say that.)
- The goat bleats from atop a crate. It followed them 200 miles. Verna's sending arrives in Rowan's head, honey-soft: *"You didn't say goodbye."*

**And the money problem, stated plainly:** ~30 gold between them, a silver a night for a bed, and no way home ever. Four famous nobodies, freshly humiliated by a city that doesn't know they killed a god. Sit in that for one beat. Now show them the shop.

## SCENE 1 — THE SHOP & THE PITCH (~25 min)

On the edge of the Great Market, between a ropemaker and a shuttered pie stand:

> **VELL'S PRACTICAL MYSTERIES**
> *Curses Read While You Wait · Reputations Managed · Dreams Brokered · RESULTS GUARANTEED*

Four cursed, broke teenagers do not walk past "Curses Read While You Wait." (If they try, the bottlecap crow lands on the sign and taps it, twice.)

**Inside:** a hoard of polished junk, framed certificates from the *Worshipful Company of Practical Mysteries* (registrar, treasurer, and entire membership: M. Vell), a bell that rings *before* the door opens, and an ordinary gecko on the wall that Martenn will not acknowledge under any circumstances. **Tobin is sprawled behind the counter** — it's muster day, the Harrow Company rotates onto the Full Fathom contract tonight, Brunna has been stomping the docks bellowing his name, and no Harrow would ever enter a shop this silly. When the door opens he leaps up and pretends to be security. He is holding a broom.

**The consultation (short, funny, a dead end on purpose):** Martenn does the full theater — brazier, gong, *invoice first* — and delivers his finding: *"Old. Elven. Extremely cursed. Beyond even my instruments."* (Three true statements.) He watches them count out their last coins from one shared purse. Four strong, scary, desperate nobodies who just got called *farmhand* on the nicest street in the city. And behind him, Tobin has gone very still, because he's counting them. There are four. Four plus two is —

**THE PLAN WALL.** Martenn locks the door, flips the sign, and pulls the curtain off the back wall: **years of heist plans.** Plan A through Plan Q. Every one annotated, crossed out, and dead for the same reason, written in increasingly emotional handwriting: **NEED SIX.** *(Plan F just says "six geese" and neither of them will discuss it.)* And in the center, lovingly maintained: **PLAN R.**

**The pitch (Ilan's scene — his brief has his goals; the shape of it):**
- **The target:** the Sanctum Bonafortunica. Every confiscated jackpot and every confiscated cheating device in the history of Larracos, in one vault. Treasure hunters bankrupt themselves just trying to learn what *quadrant* it's in.
- **The edge:** — and here Tobin, who has never once said this out loud, says it — *"It's under the Full Fathom. I've known since I was nine. The Commission of Honest Games is a shell corporation. It's* us. *It's Harrows all the way down. My family doesn't guard the Sanctum for the houses — we guard it for* ourselves*."*
- **The math:** Plan R needs six bodies in six roles. They have been two bodies for four years.
- **The loot** (what Martenn leads with): more gold than the four have ever imagined in one place.
- **The real hook (what actually lands — say it to the table's faces):** *"You've been in this city six hours and it's already decided you're nobody. Farmhands. Quarryfolk. You want to be somebody in Larracos? Nobody here remembers rich. Everybody — forever — remembers the crew that cracked the Sanctum."* Watch four people who just got looked up and down by a rich goth do that math in real time.

**CLAIMING THE ROLES (the party-formation scene — run it as an open argument over the Plan R prop, in the handouts):** the plan needs a **Face** (charms the floor), a **Gambler** (plays big, draws every eye), a **Shadow** (is never seen), a **Muscle** (moves heavy things, looks like he belongs in a doorway), an **Inside Man** (uniform, keys, rotation), and a **Broker** (runs the plan, forges the paper, does the talking downstairs). Let the table fight over who's what — miscasting themselves is comedy, and there's no wrong answer; the roles are a lens, not a law. When the six roles have names next to them, that's the act break.

**If anyone's conscience twitches:** they'd be stealing already-confiscated winnings from a secret vault owned by a shell corporation that legally doesn't exist, guarded by things that aren't employees. Even Stookin's ancestors run the ethics and come back with a shrug. This is the most consequence-free crime in Balenthas. (It is not. See: Heads. See: Tails.)

## SCENE 2 — RECON & THE MARKET (~20 min, players drive)

**Casing the Full Fathom** — the house below the tideline, where you hear the sea breathing through the walls, "which the house insists is good luck and is in fact a protection: money can only leave through one exit." Tobin sketches what he knows on the back of a certificate; the party fills the gaps in person. Give each PC **one casing activity** on the evening floor — gamble small, charm a dealer, tail the money cart, watch the guard rotation, count exits — one check each (DC 12–14), one fact per success:

1. **The tithe-slot:** every night at close, the floor manager drops one chip down a brass slot in the counting-room floor — "a tithe to the sea." The staff think it's superstition. It is a *keyhole.*
2. **The House Chip:** the tithe is always the same kind of chip — bone-white, a crown on one face, a crab on the other. Only **Floor Manager Sesta** carries them, on a chain at her waist. *(Yes: the chip's two faces are a warning. No one has ever read it as one.)*
3. **The rotation:** Harrow shift change at three bells — Brunna on the counting-room door, Dagen and Kolm walking the floor. Five soft minutes at the change.
4. **The sibling levers** (Tobin gives these free): **Dagen cries at music** and denies it. **Kolm is deeply superstitious** — he crosses himself past the tithe-slot and won't work the counting room alone. **Brunna** has no lever. Brunna is the wall.
5. **The house games** (color for the floor phase): *Tides* (dice against the tide-line), *Gull-and-Crab* (cards), crab races (the local favorite; the crabs are supplied by a man who sells them from his hat), and the standing books on the weather, the dry fountain, and the name of the mayor.
6. **The golden rule, from Tobin, deadly serious:** *"Do not win too much. Winning too much is how the house decides you're cheating, and then you and your winnings go where we are trying to break into."* (Clock this line. It's also a door — see Contingencies.)

**READ-ALOUD — the Great Market at dusk:**

> *The Great Market at dusk is a hundred arguments in six languages, all of them friendly and none of them honest. Braziers hiss against the dark. A woman sells love charms off a blanket, three coppers, made this morning, guaranteed by her own mother's grave, wherever that turns out to be. The crab-man's hat rustles. A betting tout chalks tomorrow's weather on a slate — three-to-two on rain, seven-to-one on "strange" — and takes real silver on it, from a line of people. The lamps come on one by one, in no order at all, as if the city is placing bets with itself about which street will matter tonight.*

**Then the shopping montage in the Great Market:** *"What do you buy, borrow, or steal for the job?"* Say yes and price it — evening clothes, a forged Commission inspection letter (Martenn's masterpiece), a porter's livery, lamp-oil, a live crab (three coppers, hat withdrawal optional). One bit of color if pace allows: **Ines the charm lady** presses a river-stone love charm into Ren's hand, no charge, *"for the birthday girl,"* and is serving someone else before Ren can react. She will not explain how she knew.

## SCENE 3 — THE FLOOR (~30 min)

**READ-ALOUD — descending into the Full Fathom:**

> *The Full Fathom has no ground floor. You enter at street level into a modest velvet foyer that could belong to a solicitor — and then you descend: a grand brass-railed stair spiraling down past a line etched into the wall in gilt letters, HIGH TIDE, and as you cross beneath it the air changes. Cooler. Denser. The noise of the harbor is gone, replaced by a hush like held breath — and then the floor opens below you like the inside of a jewel box. Green, everything green: hanging lamps of sea-glass, deep green felt, dealers moving between the tables like currents. Chips click like crab legs. An announcer, somewhere, is going hoarse. And underneath all of it, once you notice it you cannot stop noticing it, is the house's famous ambience — the sea itself, breathing against the outer walls, slow and patient and enormous. The house insists this is good luck. It sounds like being swallowed, gently.*

**The staff:** dealers in deep-green livery with silver wave-crests stitched at the collar, white gloves, pearl ear-studs — polite the way weather is polite. Floor men in longshore coats cut far too well, hands folded, smiles professional. Drink-runners weaving with trays of ossuvine and temple salt-wine. Harrow guards in company gray at every door, crow-and-bone crest on the breast, standing the way furniture stands. And on a low dais overlooking everything, **Floor Manager Sesta**: silver hair pinned with a fish-spine comb, the chain of bone-white House Chips at her waist, a face that has watched thirty years of human weakness and found it soothing. She does not blink often. When she does, it feels earned. She plays *Tides* against high rollers who amuse her, cheats beautifully as a courtesy, and has never once been caught — which, on this floor, is the highest credential there is.

**Who's at the tables tonight (d6, or pick — each is cover, color, or a mark):**

1. A pirate crew on shore leave, betting an entire prize-ship's worth, loudly, in the wrong order.
2. A dwarf silver-factor from the Sharabil who bets only on certainties, has lost nothing all night, and is *miserable* about it.
3. A Kettl halfling grandmother destroying the Gull-and-Crab table, cackling, method uncaught in forty years.
4. A priest of the Trickster tithing his winnings back to the tables, hand over fist. "It's a liturgy."
5. The rich goth from the arrival — it's **her hatch-day party**, a full table, garlands, a cake. Everything Ren isn't getting, done richly and worse. (Birthday runner: fire this one on purpose.)
6. A sweating Valdrek wool merchant who has been "leaving after this hand" for three days.

### THE GAMES (playable — let them sit down; the casing facts arrive between hands, and every big win feeds the Marks ladder. The games ARE the scene.)

- **TIDES** *(dice — the house classic).* A brass tide-line means seven. Stake, call **FLOOD** (over) or **EBB** (under), roll 2d6. Exactly seven is *slack water* — the sea keeps your stake. Pays even money. *How people cheat:* weighted dice, a palmed swap after the call, a confederate reading the dealer's set-down. (Sesta's personal dice roll sevens when she's bored.)
- **GULL & CRAB** *(cards — the bluffing game).* Each player draws; high card takes the pot — **unless** the low card is exactly half the high card, in which case *the crab pinches the gull* and the low card takes double. (Run it with real cards, or opposed d10s.) *How people cheat:* counting the pinch cards, sleeve work, and whatever the grandmother is doing.
- **THE CRAB DERBY** *(every hour, on the bell — the local favorite).* Six numbered crabs from the hat-man's own hat, a chalk ring, lane four ("the Chancellor") beloved and winless. Back a crab; each crab runs on 3d6; highest total pays 4:1; ties are settled the way crabs settle things. *How people cheat:* fish paste on a lane, a chilled crab (they run sluggish), whispered threats — and note well: **Ren can literally talk to crabs.** This absolutely works and is absolutely cheating.
- **THE BOOKS** *(prop bets, chalked on a slate by the cage):* tomorrow's weather (3:2 rain, 7:1 "strange") · the dry fountain running this year (400:1) · the name of the mayor (write-in; magnificent odds; never once paid out).

Whatever plan they built, run it against three simple systems:

**THE ATTENTION LADDER (instead of alarms):** the house notices in **Marks.** Botched check, visible magic, winning too big = 1 Mark each. At **1 Mark**, a dealer's eyes start following them. At **2**, a Harrow drifts their way (sibling scene — levers apply). At **3**, two floor men appear with professional smiles: *"The house requests the pleasure"* — and escort them toward the back rooms. **This is not a fail state** — the back rooms are *next to the counting room.* Every route leads down; Marks just decide how much company they have when they get there.

**THE HOUSE CHIP:** Sesta's chain, her waist, her unblinking calm. Win it (she plays *Tides* against high rollers who amuse her — and she cheats, beautifully), lift it (DC 15 Sleight of Hand; she is *watching*), swap it (Martenn's forge-work + a distraction), or charm it loose (she has run this floor thirty years; a DC 16 mountain — but Donovan is Donovan). Getting the chip is the floor phase's engine; every role has an angle on it.

**THE DESCENT:** counting room (Brunna on the door — the inspection letter, the shift change, or the vents Ren-sized only), then the **tithe-slot**: brass, coin-sized, polished by decades of nightly superstition.

**READ-ALOUD — feeding the slot:**

> *The chip goes in with a click like a key turning, because that is what it is. A section of floorboards sighs, unhitches, and swings down into dark — and the sound that rises out of it is the sea, closer now, and on the wrong side of you. A spiral stair of black iron descends through old harbor stone, and set into the walls, like fossils, are coins: hundreds of them, from mints no living person has seen, pressed into the mortar the way pilgrims press candles into shrine wax. Pale elven coins from before the Fall. Fat Dunharg trade-rounds. Coins with holes, coins with teeth-marks, one coin near the third turning that someone has lovingly polished for a century. Tide-marks ring the stairwell at intervals as you go down — which is troubling, if you think about it, because down here the tide would have to come from below. Far beneath you, the chip you paid the door lands: one clear, distant* plink. *And something catches it.*

**Set-piece triggers to fire when the room wants them:**
- **THE MOM CALL.** Martenn's daily call comes due mid-floor (his brief covers it; sputter a warlock slot as the warning). He takes it at the craps table like it's a business call, scale warming under his collar. She asks if he's eating. Then: *"Smallest of my treasures. Is that DICE I hear? Are you WAGERING?"* A dragon has opinions about gambling — chiefly that the house's hoard is badly guarded and he should be *winning* it, not renting it. His mother, it turns out, is pro-heist. Deeply, embarrassingly pro-heist.
- **THE HOLD (Tobin's moment — built, not forced).** Stress + secret magic + casino floor = at the worst moment, Tobin's suppressed wild magic *sparks* — and the Full Fathom's famous sea-glass chandelier chain parts over Brunna's head at her post. One round of fall. The only thing fast enough is the thing he's hidden his whole life: think *up*, flick the wrist. The chandelier stops in the air, green glass swaying, the whole floor staring at the runt of the Harrows holding a quarter-ton of light with two fingers. Give the table the silence. Then Brunna, cracked-voice, the only word this family knows how to mean: **"…HOLD."** *(If Joe chooses not to reveal — legitimate! — Rowan's muscle or Ren's shove saves her, the chandelier kills a card table instead, and Tobin's secret survives with a scar. Joe decides who Tobin is. Both are story.)*
- **LET REN TURN OFF THE SUN (carries into the stairs and vault):** below the floor, light is lamps — AC 12, 3 HP, one quiet shot kills a pool of it. **In darkness a gloomstalker is invisible even to darkvision.** The deeper they go, the more the dark belongs to her. The guards down there have never fought someone the dark *likes.*

## SCENE 4 — THE HONEST GAME (~15 min)

> *At the bottom of the stair: a small round room upholstered floor to ceiling in green felt, so that sound dies the moment it leaves your mouth. One hanging lamp swings gently overhead, though there is no draft — the slow roll of a ship at anchor, in a room with no ship and no sea in sight. Around a crescent table stand chairs that match nothing, not even each other: different woods, different decades, a child's stool, a captain's chair with salt-stains, a velvet monstrosity with someone's initials scratched out of the arm — the chairs of everyone who ever sat down here, none of whom left with them. Behind the table waits the dealer: brass and ivory, beautiful, roughly person-shaped, wearing a visor it does not need and sleeve garters on arms that are not arms. Behind IT, filling the far wall entirely, is a door made of one enormous coin, taller than Rowan, older than the city. The dealer shuffles. The sound is exactly like the tide. It says: "The house offers one game. Win, and pass."*

**The game is *Tides*** — call the dice high or low against the line. Simple. Fair-looking. **Rigged to the rafters:** the Croupier's dice whisper to each other, its cards remember where they're going, the felt itself is woven from confiscated marked decks. **An honest player cannot win**, tonight or ever. Each honest loss, the house collects a small stake — a copper, a button, *the memory of this morning's breakfast* (harmless, unsettling, escalating) — and deals again, forever.

**The solution: cheat.** The door only opens for craft. Any cheat works if it's sneaky or stylish — palm the die (Sleight of Hand DC 13), switch it for a market die, *mage hand* under the table, an illusion of the wrong total, Donovan charming the Croupier into looking away, Tobin flicking the die mid-air with a thought, Stookin simply picking up the Croupier's OWN whispering dice and letting them lie for the party, six people running six grifts at once. The moment the table detects craft — any craft — the Croupier stops. Racks its deck. And, with what can only be called respect, tips its visor: *"House acknowledges craft."* The great coin-door rings like a bell and rolls aside.

**Hint ladder if they stall (fire in order, ~2 min apart):** Tobin: *"the family story says no honest soul has ever entered — I always thought it was a warning…"* → Martenn (Insight DC 10): every component of this room is a confiscated cheating device; the room is MADE of cheating → a crow (who absolutely should not be down here) lands on the Croupier's shoulder and pointedly taps its sleeve, twice → Stookin's ancestors, gently: *"Everyone at this table is lying, boy. Why aren't you?"* **Hard cap:** after ~15 minutes the Croupier yawns ("Last hand. House rules.") and ANY declared cheat auto-passes. This room is a joke with a lesson, not a wall.

*(Optional grace note as the door opens: the Croupier deals each of them one card face-down as a keepsake. The cards are all the same card. There is no such card in any deck upstairs. It shows a crown on one half and a crab on the other.)*

## SCENE 5 — HEADS & TAILS (~30 min, the fight)

> *Beyond the coin-door: the vault antehall — a great drum of black stone, cold as a cellar and tall as a chapel, the sea sweating through its seams in slow silver threads. Six brass storm-lanterns hang on chains at the height of a mast, throwing overlapping circles of light on a floor mosaicked with a thousand inlaid coins — every mint, every age, pale elven silver from before the Fall pressed edge to edge with yesterday's harbor coppers, as if the city has been paying this room forever. In the center, on a raised dais, an ENORMOUS ancient coin stands upright on its edge: taller than a wagon wheel, worn nearly featureless, humming a note you feel in your back teeth. One face bears a crowned profile. The other, a crab. As you enter, the coin begins — slowly, impossibly, with the sound of a whetstone the size of a house — to spin. Two shapes step OUT of it, one from each face, the way a person steps out of a doorway. The crowned one bows, immaculate. The crab-legged one does not.*
>
> **"The house calls your wager,"** *says Heads.*
> *Tails makes a sound like a purse full of teeth.*

*(Those six storm-lanterns are Ren's: AC 12, 3 HP each, and every one she kills makes a mast-height pool of the antehall hers. Heads finds fighting in the dark undignified. Tails does not mind — but Tails has never shared a dark room with a gloomstalker, and it is about to have opinions.)*

**THE FLIP (the fight's engine):** at the top of every round (initiative 20), the great coin flips itself and lands: roll a d2 or alternate. **The face-up guardian is EMPOWERED that round** (extra attack, resistance to all damage); the face-down one is diminished (loses an attack). **The coin can be cheated:** as an action — or as a *reaction* when the coin flips — a PC within 30 ft. can try to fix the toss: Sleight of Hand, Athletics (catch/tilt it), or any clever spell/stunt, **DC 15.** Success: that player chooses the face. Fail by 5+: the coin lands on **EDGE — both guardians empowered for the round** (the house punishes sloppy cheating). Six players means someone always has a free hand for the coin — cheating the flip is the whole tactical layer, and it is exactly what this dungeon has been teaching all night.

### HEADS, the Crowned Face
*Medium construct, CR 5 — polished, polite, unbearable*
**AC** 17 · **HP** 85 (10d8+40) · **Speed** 30 ft.
**STR** 18 **DEX** 10 **CON** 18 **INT** 12 **WIS** 15 **CHA** 16
**Immune** poison, psychic; charmed, frightened, exhaustion · **Darkvision** 60 ft. (it is a coin; it has seen every kind of dark)
**Multiattack.** Two Payout strikes (three while empowered).
**The Payout.** Melee, +7, reach 5 ft., 2d8+4 bludgeoning — a ceremonial maul like a titanic coin-stamp; while empowered, +1d8 radiant (the shine).
**House Rules (Recharge 5–6).** Decree in a voice like a closing ledger: each enemy within 30 ft. makes a DC 14 Wis save or, until the end of its next turn, its speed is halved and it can't take reactions. (*"No running on the floor."*)
**Style.** Bows before striking. Narrates infractions ("Unattended beverage. Elbows on the felt."). Refers to the party's blood as "outstanding balances."

### TAILS, the Crab Face
*Medium construct, CR 5 — skittering, sideways, wrong*
**AC** 15 · **HP** 85 (10d8+40) · **Speed** 40 ft., climb 40 ft.
**STR** 14 **DEX** 18 **CON** 18 **INT** 8 **WIS** 15 **CHA** 6
**Immune** poison, psychic; charmed, frightened, exhaustion · **Darkvision** 60 ft.
**Thin as a Coin.** Tails can turn edge-on and move through any gap at least an inch wide — between crates, under doors, *through the coin-mosaic floor* — without squeezing. It uses this to vanish and reappear somewhere upsetting.
**Multiattack.** Two claw rakes (three while empowered).
**Claw Rake.** Melee, +7, reach 5 ft., 2d6+4 slashing; while empowered, +1d8 psychic (a cold pulse of pure bad luck).
**Snake Eyes (Recharge 5–6).** It rattles. One creature within 30 ft. makes a DC 14 Cha save or its luck *drains*: disadvantage on all d20 rolls until the end of its next turn.
**Style.** Never speaks. Communicates in rattles Heads translates, incorrectly, to be polite.

**Shared — Two Faces, One Coin.** If either guardian takes 30+ damage in a single round, the great coin panic-flips immediately (resolve a new flip; cheaters may react). When one guardian falls, it dissolves into a spray of coins sucked back into the great coin — and the survivor becomes **Spent**: permanently empowered, but the coin stops flipping and stands trembling on edge. When both fall, the great coin cracks down the middle, rings once like a struck bell — a note the whole city hears in its sleep — and topples flat, revealing the stair down into the vault proper.

**The yield clause (style ending / time lever):** if the party cheats the flip successfully **three rounds running**, or pulls off something so stylish the table cheers, the guardians stop mid-swing. Heads bows: *"The house acknowledges craft."* Tails rattles what is, for the first time, applause. They step back into the coin and let the crew pass. (Guardians of confiscated cheats respect exactly one thing.)

**Safety valve — the house does not kill; it CONFISCATES.** A PC dropped to 0 HP is not dying — they are *impounded*: whisked into a velvet-lined alcove in the wall, conscious, furious, and out of the fight (an ally can spend an action to yank them free, restoring them to 1 HP). If the whole party is impounded, they wake at dawn on the Full Fathom's front steps: broke, banned, alive, and legends anyway (see Contingencies). No birthday TPKs.

**Tuning:** two CR-5s against six level-5s with flip control lands hard-but-winnable. Running hot? Empowered faces get their third attack every round. Running cold? Drop House Rules/Snake Eyes recharges to 6.

## SCENE 6 — THE VAULT, THE CHUTE & THE BIRTHDAY (~30 min)

> *Down the last stair, the Sanctum Bonafortunica: a natural sea-cave dressed, with great dignity, as a counting house. There are no lamps, because none are needed — the coin lies in drifts, like snow that never melts, and it gives off a faint glow of its own, moonlight-on-water, as if ninety years of confiscated luck has to go somewhere. The air tastes of cold brass and kelp. Ledger-desks stand in a neat row where the sand meets the hoard, quills still in their stands. Gem-chests line the walls under house sigils going back generations — the Eel, the Tern, the Fathom, and older sigils belonging to houses that no longer exist, which the vault has outlived. And along the far wall, shelf after shelf of confiscated craft, all of it murmuring: dice muttering odds to each other (they are pessimists), a deck complaining about a shuffle it received in 1289, a monocle that has been reading YOU since you walked in, one (1) horseshoe labeled SECOND PLACE. Over it all, a brass placard: EVIDENCE. Below that, older, in Harrow handwriting: DO NOT TOUCH. Below that, oldest: WE MEAN IT.*

**The take (dials yours):** coin and gems worth about **500 gp each** once fenced (Martenn's cut per the shop haggle, which he *will* re-litigate), plus a velvet evidence tray labeled **"RINGS, ASSORTED — SUSPECTED"** holding exactly six **Gambler's Rings** (item card in the handouts — confiscated as suspected cheating devices, which they absolutely are). Let each player also pocket one flavor item from the cheat-shelf if they want (Cheat-Shelf table below) — quirky trinkets, DM's discretion on whether any of them ever *work.*

**The exit is the punchline of the whole city:** the Full Fathom insists money can only leave through one exit — and here it is: a polished brass **chute** marked with the house sigil, built to send confiscations *down* and, once a decade under Commission seal, tribute *up.* It takes sacks. It takes bodies if they tuck. They ride their own loot out of the vault — a full minute of screaming brass darkness — and then:

**READ-ALOUD — the payout:**

> *The chute fires you out over the shallows at the exact moment the sun clears the Arlun — six thieves and a fortune, airborne together for one perfect gold-lit instant, coins fanning around you like a jackpot the size of the sky — and then the harbor takes you, the way the harbor takes everything. You come up gasping in water the color of money. Dawn is landing on a thousand masts, one at a time. Bells are ringing somewhere: ship's bells, temple bells, and one low bell nobody can place, still humming up out of the sea beneath you. And every rooftop, every yardarm, every piling from the pier to the bone temple is lined with crows. Hundreds of them. Silent — which crows never are. Waiting. Which is when the nearest one clears its throat.*

*(Tavar: "I have never once left Larracos in the condition in which I arrived." Now they know how he feels.)*

**Aftermath beats, fast:**
- **The houses admit nothing.** The Sanctum was never robbed, because the Sanctum does not exist, because the Commission of Honest Games is just a name on a door. But the whole Salt Quarter heard a bell ring under the sea last night — and by noon, three taverns claim the crew drinks there, a stranger buys Rowan a breakfast he didn't order, and a child asks Stookin to bless his dice. **They wanted to be cool. Unconfirmable legend is the coolest thing in Larracos.**
- **The Harrow settlement (pick by table temperature):** Ansa finds them on the harbor front — everyone braces — and she looks at her soaked, rich, un-arrestable children's-crusade of a crew, and at Tobin, and says only: *"Ninety years, that vault held."* Beat. *"…Good work."* (The family literally cannot report the crime. And the runt found the flaw.) **Or quieter:** Tobin left a note on the empty ring tray: *"Found the flaw. Fix the third door. — T."* Brunna finds it on inventory day and does not show Mother.
- **Verna's 6 a.m. sending,** softly poisonous: *"You smell like money and seawater. Who is the girl in black?"* — and for the first time, Rowan doesn't answer right away.

**THEN THE CROWS CALL IT.** They've been assembling on the rooftops all scene, silent, which crows never are. The bottlecap crow flutters down, clears its throat with civic gravity, and announces to the entire harbor:

> **"BY ORDER OF THE MAYOR — LET IT BE KNOWN — TODAY IS THE HATCH-DAY OF THE QUEEN IN BLACK."**

And the crows of Larracos lose their minds.

- **The outing:** every bird in the city has known for three weeks — the Pellvar crows told the road crows told the harbor crows: *the one who throws the stones back, the one who gives us her words.* They've been leaving warm bottlecaps all night, keeping the secret badly. Ren gets to be furious, and caught, and — dripping wet, newly rich, surrounded by witnesses — she does not get to pretend she isn't pleased.
- **THE MAYOR** descends: a crow of tremendous age and circumference. He grants Ren, by croak and bottlecap seal, **Freedom of the City** — no crow will ever steal from her; every crow will carry her words. (Optional forever-gag: nobody in Larracos knows the human mayor's name, every house keeps a book on it at magnificent odds, and the crows have money down. Play it as crow self-mythology. Unless.)
- **The gifts:** the murder has spent all night building something from wire, shinies, and one feather from the Mayor's own wing — **the Murder Crown** (item card in the handouts; hers, permanent). Extra gifts from the pile (Shiny Table below).
**READ-ALOUD — the Salt Quarter annexes the morning:**

> *The news goes up the hill faster than any of you could carry it, because the crows carry it. And the Salt Quarter — which has never once stopped playing, not at dawn on a Tuesday, not at the third hour after midnight in the dead of winter — does not so much throw a party as annex one. Tables appear from nowhere. Three bands become one band without anyone agreeing on a key. Somebody rolls a barrel of ossuvine down the hill and it is caught, professionally, at the bottom, by people who have caught barrels before. Sister Marrow arrives from the bone temple with salt-wine and a cake shaped like a leviathan, pulled on a fish cart, and it is somehow excellent. Sailors who don't know why they're celebrating celebrate anyway, on principle. And through the middle of it all walks a soaked girl in black wearing a crown of wire and bottlecaps, refusing to smile in a way that every single person present correctly recognizes as delight.*

- **THE SONG.** Donovan realizes he's about to perform the first song in five years that isn't about Elowynth. Scaffold in the handouts (*"The Weird One Was Right"*) — table rule: every player hands Donovan one line for the verses, which is the whole table singing to Isabelle without breaking character. Donovan feels the glamour rise and, for the first time, *aims* it — not to be adored, but to make one specific person feel completely seen. The harbor sings the chorus back. The music, as advertised, does not stop.
- **Buttons, pick two:** Stookin's ancestors — "it has to get worse before it gets better" — say, warmly, once: *"Tonight: better."* · the gull returns the stolen tendon, nods once, respect, leaves · Ren finds the Croupier's impossible crown-and-crab card in her pocket, on the side of the crown · the goat wearing a tiny garland nobody bought it.

**Epilogue read-aloud (Tavar, closing the frame):**
> *"I was not in Larracos that spring, a fact I will regret for the remainder of a long life. But sailors repeat what they hear, and what they heard, near dawn, was a bell ringing somewhere under the sea — and what they swear to is this: that by sunrise the crows had crowned a girl in black on the harbor front, soaking wet and refusing to smile, while a bard sang something true for once and five soaked idiots emptied seawater out of a fortune. The houses deny everything, which is how you know. I have written elsewhere that Larracos does not add up. I no longer consider this a flaw in my arithmetic. — T.U."*
